# Visual system

## Design argument

CHORUS uses a luxurious dark-green prohibition-house atmosphere because the
simulation concerns social trust produced inside rooms: selective access,
polished surfaces, overheard fragments, trusted carriers, status, and the gap
between atmosphere and record. Dark academia supplies archival weight; the
prohibition-bar grammar supplies intimacy and restricted circulation; the
celluloid layer makes mediation visible.

The aesthetic must never imply that polish is truth. Source trace and social
fit remain separate measures, and glow is consistently described as fit rather
than factual support.

## Palette roles

| Role | Use |
|---|---|
| Deep green | Foremost environmental field, active glass, graph nodes, and house identity. |
| Near-black green | Stable reading surface and viewport boundary. |
| Gold or brass | Trace, archival labels, active focus accents, and selected detail. |
| Silver | Secondary serpent facet and cool structural accent; never centered as the dominant stripe. |
| Cream | Primary readable text. |
| Muted green and brown | Secondary text only after contrast verification. |

Color never determines status without text, shape, border, position, or an
accessible state attribute.

## Emblem

The CHORUS mark is a faceted low-poly letter C formed by three visibly
intertwined serpents. The reading order is green, gold, then silver, with silver
occupying the least central visual position. Bodies cross over and under one
another so the mark reads as one woven system rather than adjacent color bands.

The icon remains recognizable at favicon scale through silhouette and
interweaving, not tiny surface detail. Header and favicon use the same geometry
and color order.

## Glass

Glassmorphism is reserved for persistent house chrome and live signals. It
communicates information seen through a situated surface rather than floating
content. Active reading panes use enough opaque green-black backing to maintain
contrast over glow and film.

Glass may soften hierarchy; it may not:

- reduce text contrast;
- obscure focus;
- create two scroll layers for one task;
- make an unavailable explanation appear detached from its choice; or
- place essential information behind a hover effect.

## Celluloid and motion

Film grain, restrained frame drift, and slight registration error mark the
screen as a mediated artifact. Effects remain low-amplitude, non-flashing, and
independent of game state. Film can be disabled. System reduced-motion settings
and the Motion control suppress movement while leaving texture and information
available as appropriate.

No metric, relationship, arrival, consequence, or status is communicated only
through movement.

## Typography and density

The display face carries room titles and archival atmosphere. The interface
face carries actions and prose. The monospaced face is reserved for clocks,
codes, trace labels, compact state, and receipt metadata.

Density follows the interaction phase:

- Prelude: spacious and suggestive.
- House map: compact comparison.
- Active beat: one readable record and one bounded action set.
- Completed night: an uninterrupted readable summary, a quiet adjacent
  model-limit note, then a visually separate plain concept receipt. No tab bar,
  dashboard grid, or Heart-card taxonomy interrupts the sequence.

Text sizes may scale fluidly but retain minimum readable floors. Long generated
copy wraps; it is never clipped to protect an ornamental frame.

The naturalized summary uses a stable reading measure and ordinary paragraph
flow. Its ending receives no oversized moral, badge, or ornamental full stop:
the final thought remains the night's unresolved reach or afterimage. The
plain concept receipt may repeat a restrained entry structure for scanning,
but status and model limit remain explicit text and never depend on color or
tile position.

## Responsive priority

When space contracts, preserve in this order:

1. current artifact and action text;
2. room switcher and progress state;
3. focus, status, and accessibility semantics;
4. blocked explanation and receipts;
5. persistent navigation;
6. contextual meters;
7. ornament, film, oversized headings, and atmospheric whitespace.

Mobile is not a scaled desktop tableau. It uses the same logical viewport with
fewer simultaneous panels and stronger progressive disclosure.

## Accessibility constraints

Contrast, reduced motion, forced colors, focus, reflow, and touch behavior are
specified in [Accessibility](ACCESSIBILITY.md). A visual change is incomplete
until those states are tested. Decorative degradation is acceptable;
information loss is not.
